Wallets & Private Keys

How to store your digital assets — and why security is entirely your responsibility.

"Not Your Keys, Not Your Crypto"

In traditional banking, the bank holds your money. In crypto, you hold your own money using cryptographic "keys." This is powerful, but it means you're fully responsible for keeping them safe.

Public Key (Address)

Like your bank account number. Share this with others so they can send you crypto. It's safe to share publicly.

Private Key

Like your ATM PIN — but with no bank to call if you lose it. Whoever has this key controls the funds. Never share it.

The Seed Phrase (Recovery Phrase)

A seed phrase is a master password — usually 12 or 24 random words generated when you create a wallet. It acts as a backup for all your private keys. If you ever lose access to your wallet, the seed phrase lets you restore everything.

Crucial Rule

If you lose your seed phrase, your crypto is gone forever — there is no customer support to help you. If someone else gets your seed phrase, they can steal everything immediately.

Best Practices for Storing Your Seed Phrase
  • Write it down on physical paper immediately.
  • Store it in a fireproof safe or a secure, private location.
  • Never take a photo of it — photos can sync to the cloud.
  • Never type it into a text file, email, or cloud storage (Google Drive, iCloud, Dropbox).
  • Consider making two physical copies stored in different secure locations.

Hot vs. Cold Wallets

Hot Wallets

Software wallets connected to the internet — apps or browser extensions.

Pros: Convenient for daily use, trading, and using decentralized apps.
Cons: More vulnerable to hacks, malware, and online scams.
Examples: MetaMask, Phantom, Trust Wallet.

Cold Wallets

Physical hardware devices that store your private keys completely offline.

Pros: Extremely secure. Immune to online hacks unless you physically approve a transaction.
Cons: Costs money ($50–$150). Less convenient for frequent trading.
Examples: Ledger, Trezor, Coldcard.
Recommended Approach

Use a hot wallet for small amounts you use regularly (like a physical wallet you carry). Use a cold wallet for larger amounts you want to hold long-term (like a savings account or a safe at home).
