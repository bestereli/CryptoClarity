How to Research Crypto Projects

DYOR — Do Your Own Research — is a core crypto philosophy. Here is how to actually do it.

Before putting time or money into any cryptocurrency or token, you need to understand what it actually does, who is building it, and what the risks are. Don't buy a token just because someone on social media told you to. That's how people lose money.

Read the Whitepaper

A whitepaper is a document that explains the purpose and technology behind a project. Ask yourself:

  • What specific problem does this solve?
  • Is a blockchain actually necessary to solve it?
  • Is the document well-written, or does it feel vague?

Evaluate the Team

A great idea needs a capable team to execute it. Look for:

  • Are founders public (doxxed) or anonymous? Anonymous teams carry higher risk.
  • Do they have relevant experience in tech or cryptography?
  • Are they transparent and active in their community?

Check Tokenomics

Tokenomics = Token Economics. How is the token distributed and used?

  • What is the total supply?
  • How many tokens do founders hold? (High = risk of dumping)
  • What actually gives the token value?

Community & Development

A project is only as alive as its developers and users.

  • Check their GitHub: are developers actively writing code?
  • Is the community focused on the technology, or only the price?

Quick DYOR Checklist

The "Meme Coin" Exception

Some tokens (like Dogecoin or Pepe) have no real utility, no whitepaper, and no roadmap. They are purely driven by community sentiment and internet culture. While people do make money on them, they are highly speculative and closer to gambling than investing. Always ask yourself: is this a technology play or a sentiment play?
