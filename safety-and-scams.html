Crypto Safety & Scams

The crypto world is full of opportunities, but also predators. Learn to spot the danger before it costs you.

Common Scams to Avoid

Phishing Scams

Scammers create fake websites or send emails pretending to be a legitimate service (like an exchange or wallet provider) to trick you into entering your seed phrase or password.

Red Flag: Emails creating a false sense of urgency ("Your account will be locked!"). URLs that look slightly off — e.g., binance-security.com instead of binance.com.

Rug Pulls

Developers create a new token, hype it up to attract buyers, and then suddenly abandon the project — running away with investors' funds.

Red Flag: Anonymous teams, massive promises of guaranteed returns, and tokens where the founders hold a huge percentage of the supply.

Imposter Scams

Scammers impersonate customer support on platforms like Discord, Twitter, or Telegram. They offer to "help" you fix an issue, but they really just want your private keys.

Red Flag: Legitimate customer support will NEVER DM you first, and they will NEVER ask for your seed phrase or private keys.

Fake Exchanges & Apps

Malicious apps masquerading as legitimate trading platforms. Once you deposit funds, you can never withdraw them.

Red Flag: Apps sent to you via direct messages or sketchy ads. Always download apps from the official website only.

Golden Rules of Crypto Security

1

Never share your seed phrase. No legitimate company, app, or person will ever need it — ever.

2

If it sounds too good to be true, it is. Ignore anyone promising guaranteed high returns or risk-free investments.

3

Bookmark official URLs. Don't click Google Ads for crypto exchanges — scammers often buy ads to place fake sites at the top of search results.

4

Use a hardware wallet for large amounts. Keep only what you're actively trading on hot wallets or exchanges.

5

Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) on every exchange account, preferably using an authenticator app (not SMS).
